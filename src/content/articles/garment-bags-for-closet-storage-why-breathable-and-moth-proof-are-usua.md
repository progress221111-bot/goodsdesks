Garment bags, storage bins, and moving totes for closet use all get marketed with the same two words — "breathable" and "moth-proof" — as if a fabric can be loosely woven enough to let air through and tight enough to stop an insect that measures a few millimeters. It can't do both at once, and the five storage bins and totes below sit at different points on that spectrum depending on what the manufacturer discloses about material, closure, and build.

## What actually varies, and what doesn't matter as much as you'd think

The real variable across this category isn't color or handle count — it's what the fabric is doing structurally. A tightly woven or coated fabric blocks moisture and pests better but traps humidity inside, which is how mildew starts on stored wool or down. A loosely woven or non-woven fabric breathes, which helps with trapped moisture, but a weave open enough to pass air is also open enough to pass a clothes moth larva, which can fit through gaps smaller than a millimeter. None of the five products below publish a denier rating or thread count, so none of them can be verified as doing both jobs well — and manufacturers that claim "breathable and moth-resistant" on the same label are describing two different parts of the bag (fabric vs. zipper) without telling you which one is doing which job. What matters less than buyers assume: pack size and handle count. Six bins solve a six-drawer closet the same way regardless of brand, and handles only matter if you're carrying the bag any real distance.

## How these usually fail

Zippers are the most common point of failure in this category — tracks bend under repeated stuffing, and a bag packed past its intended volume puts lateral stress on the slider that it wasn't designed for. Non-woven fabrics, which are typical for inexpensive storage bins even when unlisted, tend to develop small tears at stress points like handle seams or corners after repeated folding and unfolding between storage seasons. A separate and well-known complaint with any sealed fabric bag is condensation: items stored slightly damp, or a closet with temperature swings, can produce visible moisture inside a closed bag that never had anywhere to evaporate to. None of this is specific to any one listing here — it's the general behavior of zippered fabric storage as a category, independent of brand.

## Before you buy

Check what you're actually storing before picking a bag type. Wool, down, and other natural fibers are the materials moths target, and if you're storing those in a bag without a verified tight weave or sealed liner, pair it with cedar or a lavender sachet regardless of what the label claims — don't rely on the bag alone. Check your storage space's humidity, not just its temperature: a basement or garage that runs damp for part of the year favors a bag with better airflow over a fully sealed one, even if that means accepting more pest risk and compensating with a repellent. Measure the item stack before buying on capacity claims like the 90L figure above, since a bag rated by volume can still fail to close over an irregularly shaped item like a stuffed comforter. And if the bags will get carried, not just shelved, prioritize the ones that specify handle reinforcement or zipper strength, since those are the two points most likely to fail under the stress of being moved rather than left alone in a closet.
